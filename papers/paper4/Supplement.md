## S1. Data, Partitions and Protocol Details

### A. N-BaIoT preparation

N-BaIoT contains the traffic of nine commercial IoT devices (two doorbells, a thermostat, a baby monitor, four security cameras and a webcam), recorded while benign and while infected with the BASHLITE (gafgyt) and Mirai botnets, and described by {{NB_nfeat}} statistics of the packet streams computed by the Kitsune feature extractor. The full data have {{NB_nrows_full}} records. We draw a uniform {{NB_frac}}% sample of every device–class file (seed 42), which keeps every device's class mix, and remove duplicates at the 32-bit floating-point precision the model sees ({{NB_ndup}} records; at 64-bit precision most of them look distinct), and {{NB_nconf}} records whose feature vector carries two labels. The BASHLITE TCP and UDP floods saturate the stream statistics: their sampled records collapse to {{NB_dropped}}, so no split can keep their test records unseen in training, and we drop both classes. The remaining {{NB_nclasses}} classes are benign traffic, three BASHLITE and five Mirai attacks; {{NB_n_nomirai}} devices ({{NB_nomirai}}) were not infected with Mirai. The records are split 70/15/15 stratified by device and class, zero-variance features are dropped and min–max scaling is fitted on the training split, without feature selection, and a guard removes the {{NB_guard_val}} validation and {{NB_guard_test}} test records that are still identical to a training record (features and label) after scaling. This gives {{NB_ntrain}} training, {{NB_nval}} validation and {{NB_ntest}} test records. Because the stream statistics of neighbouring packets overlap, a random split still makes absolute N-BaIoT scores optimistic; the comparisons in the paper are between methods trained and tested on the same split.

### B. Partitions

We describe a client's label skew by the share of its two largest classes and the number of classes that reach 1% of its records. Over the five repetitions on TON-IoT, the two largest classes make up {{TON_skew_a05_top2}} of a client's data at α = 0.5 (mean {{TON_skew_a05_top2_mean}}, {{TON_skew_a05_cls_mean}} classes on average) and {{TON_skew_a01_top2}} at α = 0.1 (mean {{TON_skew_a01_top2_mean}}, {{TON_skew_a01_cls_mean}} classes). Under the host partition, which assigns each of the {{TON_n_hosts}} destination hosts of the training split as a whole to the currently smallest of four clients, in a fresh random order per repetition, the two largest classes make up {{TON_skew_natr_top2}} (mean {{TON_skew_natr_top2_mean}}; {{TON_skew_natr_cls_mean}} classes). The largest host receives {{TON_host1_share}}% of the training records and the ten largest {{TON_host10_share}}%, so client sizes vary from {{TON_natr_size_min}} to {{TON_natr_size_max}} records. On N-BaIoT the devices hold {{NB_dev_min}} to {{NB_dev_max}} training records; the two largest classes make up {{NB_skew_dev_top2}} of a device's data ({{NB_skew_dev_cls_mean}} classes on average), against {{NB_skew_a05_N9_top2_mean}} and {{NB_skew_a01_N9_top2_mean}} on average for Dirichlet partitions over nine clients with α = 0.5 and 0.1.

### C. Training details

Round selection uses the pooled validation split; in a deployment each client would report the confusion counts on its own validation records, which the server sums to the same pooled score. Local-only models are selected on the pooled validation set, which favours models that generalise beyond the client's own traffic and therefore, if anything, disadvantages local models on their own traffic. In every epoch the last incomplete batch is dropped (training is compiled with a fixed batch size); the dropped records differ between epochs. GPU training with XLA is not bit-wise deterministic, so a repetition reproduces its partition and initialisation but not every training step; re-running identical configurations ({{RERUN_N}} pairs) changed the validation-selected test macro-F1 by {{RERUN_MEANABS}} on average (SD of the signed differences {{RERUN_SD}}) and by up to {{RERUN_MAXDIFF}}. We did not try TensorFlow's deterministic mode (TF_DETERMINISTIC_OPS without XLA), which would slow training. For reference, a constant predictor of the majority class scores a macro-F1 of {{TON_majority_f1}} on TON-IoT and {{UNSW_majority_f1}} on UNSW-NB15. The centralised reference keeps its Adam state across epochs; it and the local-only models are evaluated and selected every two epochs (50 checkpoints), so a selected "round" of the centralised model counts two epochs.

### E. DP-SGD details and communication

A DP step whose Poisson draw is empty is skipped (the accounting treats every step as released), and the noise comes from TensorFlow's standard, not cryptographically secure, generator. The guarantee assumes that the preprocessing statistics, the client sizes and the hyperparameters, all derived from the training data without privacy, are public; it does not hide which client participated. A model update for TON-IoT occupies {{TON_model_kb}} kB, so a round with four clients transfers {{TON_fedavg_a05_mb_round}} MB and 50 rounds {{TON_fedavg_a05_mb}} MB (SCAFFOLD twice as much), more than the {{TON_raw_mb}} MB of the preprocessed TON-IoT training matrix: FL does not save bandwidth over a one-off upload; its benefit is that the traffic never leaves the site.

## S7. Differential Privacy

The DP-SGD code without clipping or noise is close to non-private FedAvg on the same schedule on TON-IoT ({{TON_dp_noclip_nonoise_sel}} against {{TON_fedavg_gn_short_sel}}; the two differ in dropout and in Poisson versus shuffled batches), and clipping alone costs little in macro-F1 ({{TON_dp_clip_nonoise_sel}}), although Password already loses (Fig. 3). On UNSW-NB15 (Table {{tabref:UNSW_dp}}, Fig. S1) noise costs {{UNSW_dp_cost_short_06}} to {{UNSW_dp_cost_short_25}} points against same-schedule non-private training for ε between {{UNSW_dp_sigma25_eps_mean}} and {{UNSW_dp_sigma06_eps_mean}} (the smaller clients give larger ε for the same noise); the backdoor and DoS classes, weak even without noise, and shellcode ({{UNSW_shell_nonoise}} without noise) disappear, generic and reconnaissance traffic lose up to about ten points, and benign, exploit and fuzzer traffic at most five; the analysis and worms classes are near zero in every DP configuration. On TON-IoT the rare MITM class ({{TON_ntrain_mitm}} training records) is detected poorly and erratically under noise: its F1 is {{TON_mitm_nonoise}} without noise, {{TON_mitm_s02}} and {{TON_mitm_s03}} at σ = 0.2 and 0.3, {{TON_mitm_s06}} at σ = 0.6 and {{TON_mitm_s1}} at σ = 1, and zero from σ = 1.5. Against non-private FedAvg on the same schedule rather than the DP code without clipping or noise, {{TON_dp_nlost_06_np}} attack classes lose at least ten points at σ = 0.6 and {{TON_dp_nlost_25_np}} at σ = 2.5. At σ = 0.2 (ε ≈ {{TON_dp_sigma02_eps}}) macro-F1 is {{TON_dp_sigma02_sel}}, {{TON_dp_cost_short_02}} points below non-private training, and at σ = 0.3 {{TON_dp_sigma03_sel}}. The full budget has no clipping-only run, so the effect of clipping alone at 50 × 2 (relevant for Password) is not known.

DP tuning on TON-IoT at σ = 1 ({{TON_dpt_n_word}} repetitions each, on seeds of the reported repetitions, so these test scores are not independent of the tuning; the privacy cost of tuning is not accounted): clipping norms of 0.5, 2 and 4 instead of 1 give {{TON_dpt_C05_sel}}, {{TON_dpt_C2_sel}} and {{TON_dpt_C4_sel}}, so the best clipping norm lies inside the grid, but C = 2 scores slightly above the C = 1 of the main runs, so the reported DP cost may be overstated by one to two points; learning rates of 0.001 and 0.006 give {{TON_dpt_lr0001_sel}} and {{TON_dpt_lr0006_sel}}; and doubling the schedule to 40 rounds gives {{TON_dpt_r40_sigma1_sel}} at ε = {{TON_dpt_r40_sigma1_eps}}, still {{TON_dp_gap_r40}} points below the same code without noise or clipping ({{TON_dpt_r40_nonoise_sel}}). The DP configuration was tuned on TON-IoT at α = 0.5 only; under severe skew, small clients would have a sampling rate close to one and a much larger ε for the same noise.

Because most of the cost appears at negligible noise, we checked whether it comes from Adam, which rescales every coordinate of the noisy gradient by its running magnitude, or from the batch size, the main lever of DP-SGD.
